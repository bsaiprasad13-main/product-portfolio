## Finding product management (why PM)
In his seventh semester, a "What is a product manager?" video appeared in his YouTube feed. He learned that PMs focus on the "why", not the "how": why we are building this, why this and not that, understanding users, and understanding the business. He realized this was exactly what he had been chasing since his first year: talking to people, understanding what they need, and convincing them of a solution backed by user sense, business sense, and engineering. He didn't jump in blindly: he researched what the role is, what the day-to-day work looks like, and what topics he would need to learn, and checked it against what he already enjoyed: handling people, talking to people, convincing them, and seeing things from different perspectives.

## The extension-year decision
He had two options: (1) prepare for software placements and finish his remaining courses in one year, or (2) extend his B.Tech by a year, finish courses over two years, and prepare properly for product roles. He chose the extension. It was a calculated risk, guided by a principle he has held throughout college: make decisions he won't regret years later. He started learning PM with whatever was available: YouTube channels, guidance from people he knew, and a product management course at college that taught him the fundamentals of user research, problem framing, interviewing users, drawing insights from research, and shaping solutions. ## CampusGuide: what it is and results
CampusGuide (founder) is a 0-to-1 student knowledge base and community platform on Discord with a real backend. One place with organized categories and channels covering scholarships, college teams, hostel and campus facilities, and how the college system works, so students know what to do next, whom to contact, and what to choose. It reached 260+ users in 3 weeks through a freshers-first launch, and later expanded to projects, referrals, and direct alumni hiring. He sees it as a starting point, not a finished product. Link: https://campusguide.site/join

## CampusGuide: user research and the insight
In his fourth year he realized that over three years he had met lots of people, but his real exposure to what the college offered was low: quantity of contacts, not quality of information. For example, he only learned in his fourth year that there was a product-focused team at college. Instead of assuming everyone had his problem, he tested it: he ran 30+ focused one-on-one interviews with first, second, and third-year students and alumni, all aimed at one question: what is the main problem students face? He took written notes on the key insights from each interview (not audio recordings), then looked across them for common themes. He heard many problems: finding resources for exam preparation, not knowing how to prepare for internships, not knowing what to prepare, not knowing which teams to join, and struggling to manage everything. The common root: students weren't getting wrong information, they weren't getting enough of the right information at the right time. Most students didn't fully know what the college offered.

## CampusGuide: building, launch, and tech
His first idea was an AI chatbot, but he had a hard budget constraint and paying for LLM calls on every question wasn't sustainable, so he pivoted to a simpler solution: a Discord server. To keep it trusted, he built a verification layer: students verify with their IITM student email via an OTP before getting full access. He collected and organized the information in June, built the verification system in July, and launched through WhatsApp groups, LinkedIn, and freshers who shared it with others. After launch he kept iterating on the server's structure (categories and channels) based on early feedback. Tech: a 3-service platform on Railway (FastAPI backend, Discord bot, analytics database) with no data shared between services, and privacy-first analytics tracking daily, weekly, and monthly active users with no messages stored. Stack: FastAPI, PostgreSQL, OAuth, Railway, Cloudflare.

## Free Rooms

He built it using AI tools, including Claude: a room-availability system tracking 135 rooms across 18 buildings using real timetable data, with building, day, and time-slot filters. He launched near the end of a semester, so usage naturally dropped as the semester ended; he took that as a lesson and planned to make it better for the next semester. Stack: PWA, GA4, GitHub. ## Mutual Fund FAQ Assistant
A technical project: a RAG chatbot that grounds mutual fund answers in real fund data so it doesn't hallucinate. A 3-layer privacy check refuses personal-data requests (on the input, with a classifier, and on the output). Guardrails block investment advice and prompt-injection attempts. Pipeline: Playwright scraping, BGE embeddings, ChromaDB, FastAPI, Groq/Llama-3. Tested against 7 sets of factual and adversarial edge-case questions.

## Weekly Product Review Pulse
A technical project: an AI agent that scrapes 5000+ Play Store reviews and clusters them by theme (UMAP + HDBSCAN). Groq cleans and normalizes the raw reviews; Gemini 2.0 Flash classifies them and summarizes insights grounded in real quotes. A custom MCP server connects it to Google Docs and Gmail so reports and alerts go out automatically. It removes personal data (phone numbers, emails, UPI IDs) first. The agent and MCP server run on Zoho Catalyst 3.0.

## Jarvis
A technical project: a daily AI news briefing. Every morning it pulls new stories from 7 tech news sources (Product Hunt, Hacker News, TechCrunch, VentureBeat, YourStory, Inc42, Business Standard), and Gemini Flash writes a digest grounded only in those stories, sent by email with Resend. It skips stories sent in the last 14 days and near-duplicates (compared by text similarity). Self-healing: if a feed moves or blocks requests, it tries alternative URLs and a proxy fallback and saves the working URL; failed sources are flagged in the digest, and run failures trigger an alert email. Runs in Python on Zoho Catalyst 3.0 (Cron + Data Store) at zero infrastructure cost. It does not use a vector database or ChromaDB. GitHub: https://github.com/bsaiprasad13-main/JARVIS-AI-NEWS-REPORTER

## TalkToMe
A technical project: an Android app for Indian-language voice typing. You speak Telugu and it types your exact words in casual English letters (for example "ekkada unnav?") straight into WhatsApp or any chat. It transliterates; it doesn't translate the meaning. Built for a gap he faces daily: keyboards either write Telugu script or translate to English, and even Wispr Flow only offers romanized output for Hindi. A floating mic bubble appears only when the keyboard is open (via Android's Accessibility Service) and inserts text at the cursor, falling back to paste or clipboard. He built a custom audio pipeline (raw 16 kHz PCM with a hand-written WAV header) after Sarvam AI's API rejected Android's compressed recordings. Accept/reject buttons keep the user in control; the last 10 transcripts are saved on the phone. Stack: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Sarvam AI's speech model, Accessibility Service. GitHub: https://github.com/bsaiprasad13-main/TalkToMe

## Sensy
A technical project: an Android app that auto-replies to missed calls. Set a status like "Gym" or "Sleeping" for a set time, and it instantly texts anyone whose call you miss with a personalized message. It runs in the background with Foreground Services, with a home-screen widget for 1-tap status updates. Fully on-device: no cloud or third-party APIs, so it's private and free to run. Stack: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose. GitHub: https://github.com/bsaiprasad13-main/Sensy

## How this chatbot works (the AI version of Sai on this website)
This chat on Sai's portfolio website, the one the visitor is talking to right now, is an AI version of Sai that he built himself. It is different from his Mutual Fund FAQ Assistant project. It runs on a Cloudflare Worker that calls Groq (OpenAI's GPT-OSS models). It uses retrieval: Sai's knowledge is split into topic sections, each question is embedded with Cloudflare Workers AI (BGE embeddings), and only the most relevant sections are sent to the model along with a short core profile, which keeps answers grounded and fast within free-tier limits.
