// 01 — How I got here

I used college as a testing ground, trying things until something clicked. In my first and second year, I leaned into coding and liked it, but I was mostly building what others asked me to, without much room to think for myself. In my third year, I moved to design, hoping for more creative freedom. It helped, but I noticed design was mostly about making things look and feel right, without really digging into the problem behind it.

It was only in my fourth year that I discovered product management, and it felt different right away. It wasn't just one skill. It needed all of them together: understanding the user, the business, the market, and the engineering behind it. That mix is what pulled me in.

Thinking back, I'd already been doing this without realizing it: leading the Safety & Security team at Saarang, working in project management at CFI, and captaining my cricket team. All of it came down to the same things: taking ownership, working with people, and staying calm under pressure.

By my fourth year, I knew product was the right path, but I wasn't ready to walk into the corporate world and compete with confidence. So I took a big risk. I added an extra year to my degree, just to get properly ready for product roles. I'm in that year now, and it's been worth it.

Summary: I tried a lot of things before I found the one that made sense to me.
Takeaway: Give everything to what you're doing. That's how you find what you like.
