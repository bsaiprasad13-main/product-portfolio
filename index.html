The story behind this page 2026

To whoever's reading this before an interview, a review, or just out of curiosity.

Since my first year, I have cared more about fixing problems that actually bother people, and I have always been better at doing that through people than through a keyboard. I first thought coding was the way to do this, so I spent my first two years there. Then I thought design was the answer, so I explored that in my third year. It was only in my fourth year, when I found product management, that everything made sense. This was exactly what I had been chasing since the beginning.

Looking back, the same pattern shows up everywhere. Leading Safety and Security at Saarang, managing projects at CFI, and captaining my cricket team all needed the same things: understanding people, taking ownership, and staying calm under pressure. That is the real thread running through this page, not just the projects below.

// 01 — How I got here

01
Same want, wrong tool: code

From year one, I was drawn to problems worth solving, the kind with a real pain point behind them. In my first two years I leaned into coding, thinking it was the way to get there. I liked it, but I was mostly building what others asked me to, without the room to talk to people and shape the problem myself.

02
Same want, another tool: design

Third year, I moved to design, thinking that was it instead. It got me closer to people and their actual experience, but design was still mostly about making things look and feel right, not about understanding and convincing the people on the other side of the problem.

03
The real name for it: product

Fourth year, I found product management and it clicked immediately. This was what I'd been chasing since year one: talking to people, understanding what they actually needed, and convincing them of a solution, backed by the user sense, the business sense, and the engineering to make it real.

04
Betting a year on it

I knew product was the right path, but I wasn't ready to compete with confidence. So I took a risk and added an extra year to my degree just to get properly ready. I'm in that year now, and it's been worth it.

Turns out I'd already been doing this without realizing it, leading Safety and Security at Saarang, project management at CFI, and captaining my cricket team. Same habits every time: ownership, working with people, staying calm under pressure.
